01

Approaching a value

Limits

Definition
$$\lim_{x\to a}f(x)=L$$

means that $f(x)$ can be made arbitrarily close to $L$ by taking $x$ sufficiently close to $a$.

Why limits matter

Calculus studies continuous change. Limits give us a precise language for talking about values that a function approaches, even when the function is undefined at the point itself.

Example

Evaluate

$$\lim_{x\to 2}\frac{x^2-4}{x-2}$$

Factor the numerator:

$$\frac{(x-2)(x+2)}{x-2}=x+2\quad (x\ne2)$$

Therefore, the limit is $4$.

Limit checklist

  1. Substitute first. If the result is finite, you are done.
  2. Factor or rationalize when you get an indeterminate form like $0/0$.
  3. Compare one-sided limits for piecewise functions.
  4. Use dominant terms when $x\to\infty$.
A limit describes nearby behavior. The actual value $f(a)$ can be different—or may not exist at all.
02

Instantaneous change

Derivatives

Definition
$$f'(x)=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}$$

The derivative is the slope of the tangent line and the instantaneous rate of change.

$$\frac{d}{dx}x^n=nx^{n-1}$$

$$\frac{d}{dx}(uv)=u'v+uv'$$
Chain rule
$$\frac{d}{dx}f(g(x))=f'(g(x))g'(x)$$

Accumulation and area

Integrals

Riemann sum
$$\int_a^b f(x)\,dx=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(x_i^*)\Delta x$$

An integral is the limiting sum of many tiny contributions.
